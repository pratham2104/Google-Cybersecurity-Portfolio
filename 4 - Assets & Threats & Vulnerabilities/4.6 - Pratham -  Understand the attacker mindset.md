# Parking Lot USB Drive  

## Scenario  
As a member of the security team at Rhetorical Hospital, you arrive one morning and notice a USB stick lying on the ground in the parking lot with the hospital’s logo on it. Since no one else is around, you pick it up to examine.  

Back in your office, you decide to analyze the drive using the team’s virtualization software. Virtualization is chosen because it allows investigation in an isolated environment—separating the USB contents from the hospital’s real network and files. This ensures that even if the device contains malware, it cannot affect other systems.  

Upon inspection, the drive contains both personal and professional files. For example, there are folders with family and pet photos, as well as sensitive work-related documents such as a new hire letter and an employee shift schedule. The mix of personal and organizational data raises concerns about how an attacker might exploit such information, and whether the entire situation could have been intentionally staged as part of a social engineering attempt.  

## Solution  

| Points           | Description |  
| ---------------- | ----------- |  
| **Contents**     | While not every document holds sensitive personal data, Jorge would still prefer that his personal and family-related information remain private. The work-related files, however, include sensitive operational details of the hospital, such as staffing schedules and HR documents, which could be valuable to attackers. |  
| **Attacker Mindset** | An attacker with access to both personal and work-related files could easily craft convincing phishing attacks targeting Jorge. They could use the personal details to impersonate someone Jorge knows, or leverage hospital-related information to send realistic, malicious work emails—potentially leading to identity theft or further system compromise. |  
| **Risk Analysis** | Preventative measures must be applied at multiple levels. From a managerial perspective, employees should be trained to never insert unknown USB devices and to immediately report suspicious findings. From an operational standpoint, regular antivirus scans and monitoring are necessary. On the technical side, disabling the **autoplay** feature ensures malicious code doesn’t execute automatically. Additionally, files should only be exchanged through approved secure channels that employ email scanning and antivirus tools before being opened. |
