## Scenario 1
A small U.S. health care clinic specializing in delivering primary-care services experienced a security incident on a Tuesday morning, at approximately 9:00 a.m. Several employees reported that they were unable to use their computers to access files like medical records. Business operations shut down because employees were unable to access the files and software needed to do their job.

Additionally, employees also reported that a ransom note was displayed on their computers. The ransom note stated that all the company's files were encrypted by an organized group of unethical hackers who are known to target organizations in healthcare and transportation industries. In exchange for restoring access to the encrypted files, the ransom note demanded a large sum of money in exchange for the decryption key. 

The attackers were able to gain access into the company's network by using targeted phishing emails, which were sent to several employees of the company. The phishing emails contained a malicious attachment that installed malware on the employee's computer once it was downloaded.

Once the attackers gained access, they deployed their ransomware, which encrypted critical files. The company was unable to access critical patient data, causing major disruptions in their business operations. The company was forced to shut down their computer systems and contact several organizations to report the incident and receive technical assistance.

## Scenario 2
You recently joined the security team as a level-one Security Operations Center (SOC) analyst at a mid-sized retail company. Along with its physical store locations, your company also conducts operations in e-commerce, which account for 80% of its sales.  
Recently, the company experienced a major security incident involving a data breach of over one million users. To prevent future incidents, you have been asked to review the final report to understand the life cycle of the incident.

---

# Report
| **Date:** December 28, 2022 | **Entry:** #1 |
|------------------------------|---------------|
| **Description** | Reviewing a major data breach incident at a mid-sized retail company that compromised customer personal and financial information due to a web application vulnerability. |
| **Tool(s) used** | Web application access logs, vulnerability scanning tools, and incident investigation methods. |
| **The 5 W's** | • **Who** caused the incident? An unknown external attacker exploiting a web application vulnerability.<br><br>• **What** happened? The attacker performed a forced browsing attack, modifying order numbers in the e-commerce web application to access unauthorized customer transaction data and exfiltrate personal identifiable information (PII).<br><br>• **When** did the incident occur? The attack was discovered on **December 28, 2022, at 7:20 p.m. PT**, after initial extortion emails were received beginning **December 22, 2022**.<br><br>• **Where** did the incident happen? On the company’s **e-commerce web application**.<br><br>• **Why** did the incident happen? Due to a **vulnerability in the web application** that allowed access to customer order confirmation pages without proper authentication. |
| **Response actions** | • Security team initiated an on-site investigation to confirm and mitigate the vulnerability.<br>• Collaborated with public relations to notify customers about the breach.<br>• Offered free identity protection services to affected users.<br>• Conducted forensic analysis of web logs to determine the scope of the data accessed. |
| **Future recommendations** | • Perform **routine vulnerability scans** and **penetration testing**.<br>• Implement **access control mechanisms** such as allowlisting to restrict access to authorized URLs.<br>• Require **user authentication** for accessing sensitive content.<br>• Enhance employee **incident reporting procedures** to ensure faster escalation of suspicious emails. |
| **Additional notes** | The attacker demanded cryptocurrency ransom, escalating from $25,000 to $50,000. The incident caused approximately **$100,000 in direct costs** and potential revenue loss. This emphasizes the need for proactive vulnerability management, employee phishing awareness, and improved monitoring of web traffic for abnormal activity. |

## Scenario 3

### Overview
You are a Level-One Security Operations Center (SOC) Analyst at a **financial services company**.  
Recently, an alert was triggered regarding a **suspicious file download** on an employee’s workstation. After investigation, it was discovered that the employee had received an email containing a **password-protected spreadsheet**. The email also provided the password to open the file.  

Once the employee downloaded and opened the spreadsheet, a **malicious payload** executed on the system. You proceeded to investigate the file’s hash to verify whether it was malicious and to identify additional indicators of compromise (IoCs) using **VirusTotal**.
