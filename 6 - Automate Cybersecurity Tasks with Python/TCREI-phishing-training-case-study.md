**Author:** Pratham Agarwal
**Date:** July 2026  
**Project:** Workplace Phishing & Malware Employee Training Module  

---

## 📖 Introduction & Scenario
Recently, employees across our organization reported receiving a wave of sophisticated phishing emails trying to trick them into revealing sensitive credentials. Recognizing the risk, my manager tasked me with training the team to better identify phishing indicators and physical signs of malware. 

To execute this quickly and professionally, I chose to collaborate with a Generative AI tool. However, instead of asking for a generic response, I utilized the **T-C-R-E-I Prompting Framework** to iteratively engineer a world-class, production-ready corporate reference guide and an engaging 5-minute team meeting talking script. 

This case study documents exactly how I applied each stage of the framework to transform a weak initial AI response into highly impactful training materials.

---

## 🛠️ The T-C-R-E-I Framework Breakdown & My Iterative Process

### 1. 🧠 Thoughtfully (Describe the task, specifying a persona and format preference)
*   **The Concept:** Before touching the keyboard, I defined *who* the AI should be and *what layout* I needed. A generic assistant writes textbook definitions; a corporate trainer writes actionable materials.
*   **My Initial Execution:** I started with a simple baseline test to see what a raw prompt would yield.
    *   *Baseline Prompt:* `"Write a reference guide on phishing emails and malware."`
    *   *The Problem:* The output was a generic text-heavy wall. It defined the terms accurately but completely lacked visual hierarchy, real-world relevance, or clear calls to action for an internal workforce.

### 2. 🏗️ Create (Include any context the gen AI tool might need)
*   **The Concept:** AI requires clear environmental context to tailor its vocabulary, tone, and depth to the target end-user.
*   **My Context Injection:** I informed the tool that our company was actively experiencing a phishing spike. Crucially, I specified the audience: non-technical employees in HR, Sales, and Operations. This signaled the AI to maintain a warm, alert, and supportive tone while strictly avoiding dense engineering jargon.

### 3. 🔍 Really (Add references the gen AI tool can use to inform its output)
*   **The Concept:** Ground the AI with explicit criteria, real-world indicators, and concrete workplace rules to prevent vague or hallucinated instructions.
*   **My Constraints:** I ordered the system to reference three specific pillars:
    1. The "Anatomy of a Phishing Email" explicitly tracking false urgency, name spoofing, and generic greetings.
    2. Exactly 5 hardware/system signs of an active malware infection.
    3. Our exact internal security protocol: using the integrated "Report Phishing" button or calling the hotline at **Ext. 5555**.

### 4. ⭐ Excellent (Evaluate the output to identify opportunities for improvement)
*   **The Concept:** Never accept the first comprehensive draft. Critique it like a senior editor looking at scannability, pacing, and visual layout.
*   **My Evaluation:** When the AI provided a structured draft, I analyzed it and noticed it was still a bit text-heavy for a busy employee to read. I also realized I needed a verbal way to present this information to my team in our next sync.

### 5. ⌨️ Inputs (Iterate on your initial prompt to attain those improvements)
*   **The Concept:** Feed the evaluation notes back into the model to refine, compress, and generate secondary assets.
*   **My Final Master Prompt:**
    ```text
    Act as an expert corporate Cybersecurity Awareness Trainer. Write a highly scannable, short-form reference guide and a corresponding 5-minute meeting script for non-technical employees (HR, Sales, Operations). 
    
    Context: Our company is seeing an uptick in phishing. Keep the tone supportive and alert. 
    
    Requirements:
    1. Section 1: Break down the "Anatomy of a Phish" using 3 red flags (False Urgency, From Name Trap, Generic Greetings). Add a placeholder for a screenshot.
    2. Section 2: List exactly 5 signs of malware (slowdowns, pop-ups, mysterious apps, mouse moving, battery/fan drain).
    3. Action Plan: Include steps to use our "Report Phishing" toolbar button and call Ext. 5555.
    4. Script: Provide a 5-minute meeting script walking through this exact guide. Keep the combined guide under 500 words for a one-page layout. Use clean Markdown format.
    ```

## 🎯 The Final Production-Ready Output

Below is the definitive, high-impact output achieved through this exemplary prompting methodology.

### 📌 Section A: Employee Security Guide (One-Page Cheat Sheet)

Phishing and malware are the two most common ways cybercriminals try to slip past our defenses. This quick reference guide will help you spot suspicious activity and keep our corporate network safe.

#### 🎣 1. The Anatomy of a Phishing Email
Phishing emails are fake messages designed to look like they come from trusted sources to trick you into sharing credentials or downloading malicious files. Pause and look for these **three red flags**:

*   **🚨 False Urgency:** Pressuring you to act immediately (e.g., *"Your account will be deactivated in 24 hours"*).
*   **📧 The "From" Display Name Trap:** The name says "IT Support," but the actual email address is from a random domain (e.g., `support@it-update-secure.com`).
*   **🔗 Generic Greetings & Links:** Greetings like *"Dear Customer"* or weirdly long, misspelled links when you hover your mouse over them.

#### 💻 2. 5 Signs Your Computer Has Malware
If your workstation starts behaving strangely, look for these indicators:
1.  **Extreme Slowdowns:** Your apps or internet suddenly crawl at a snail's pace.
2.  **Unwanted Pop-ups:** Strange ads or fake security warnings blink on your screen.
3.  **Mysterious New Apps:** Toolbars or software appear that you never downloaded.
4.  **The "Ghost in the Machine":** Your mouse moves on its own, or your browser redirects to unfamiliar sites.
5.  **Rapid Battery/Fan Drain:** Your laptop gets intensely hot or the battery drains rapidly while idle.

#### 🛑 What to Do Next
If an email or machine behavior looks suspicious, **do not click links, open attachments, or reply.**
1.  **Do Not Interact:** Do not attempt to forward the email normally to colleagues, as this can spread the threat.
2.  **Click the Report Phishing Button:** Use the **"Report Phishing"** button in your email client toolbar to securely alert our security operations center.
3.  **Contact IT Helpdesk:** For suspected malware, disconnect Wi-Fi immediately and call the Security Hotline at **Ext. 5555**.

### 🎤 Section B: 5-Minute Team Meeting Talking Script

*   **0:00 – 1:00 | The Hook**
    "Hey everyone, before we jump into our usual agenda, let's take five minutes to talk about keeping our team safe from a recent uptick in sneaky phishing emails hitting our inboxes. Thank you to everyone who has flagged them so far! I’ll be sending over a one-page cheat sheet right after this meeting. We just want to make sure everyone has the basic 'radar' to spot these traps before clicking."
*   **1:00 – 2:30 | Anatomy of a Phish**
    "When you open any email, look for three simple red flags. First, **False Urgency**—if it screams 'act now or get locked out,' pause. Scammers rely on panic. Second, the **From Name Trap**—the display name might say 'IT Support,' but if you hover over the address, it's a random look-alike domain. Third, look out for **Generic Greetings** like 'Dear Employee' instead of your name."
*   **2:30 – 3:45 | Signs of Malware**
    "The second half covers malware. If you click something by accident, your computer might start acting possessed. Watch out for agonizingly slow performance, sudden random pop-ups, or your laptop getting intensely hot and draining battery while you're barely using it. Don't ignore it—let us know."
*   **3:45 – 5:00 | Wrap-Up & Action Steps**
    "If an email looks off, **do not click, reply, or forward it to your teammates** (as that can spread it). Instead, click the integrated **'Report Phishing' button** right in your email toolbar. It deletes the email and safely alerts security. If your machine is already acting up, pull the plug on your Wi-Fi and call the hotline at **Ext. 5555**. I'm dropping the link to this guide in our team chat now—please bookmark it!
