## Scenario 

As a security analyst at a social media company, you are investigating a serious data breach that recently occurred. This breach exposed sensitive customer details, including names and addresses, putting user privacy at risk. In response, the company is aiming to strengthen its network defenses through the adoption of consistent and proactive hardening practices that can help avoid similar incidents in the future.

During your assessment of the network, you identify four significant security gaps:

1. Employees are sharing their passwords.
2. The administrator account for the database is still using its factory-set default password.
3. There are no firewall rules configured to regulate inbound and outbound network traffic.
4. The system lacks multifactor authentication (MFA) for user access.

If these weaknesses are not addressed, the organization remains highly vulnerable to future data breaches or other forms of cyberattack. To mitigate these risks, you are tasked with creating a security risk assessment that evaluates the incident and proposes effective network hardening strategies.

## Part 1: Select up to Three Hardening Tools and Methods to Implement

Based on the vulnerabilities discovered in the social media organization’s network, the following three network hardening tools and methods are recommended for immediate implementation:

### 1. **Password Policies**
Enforce strong password policies across the organization based on NIST guidelines. This includes using password hashing and salting techniques and educating employees on not reusing or sharing passwords. This will directly address the issue of employees sharing passwords and the use of default admin credentials.

### 2. **Firewall Maintenance and Port Filtering**
Update and enforce strict firewall rules to filter incoming and outgoing traffic. Unused and unmonitored ports should be disabled or filtered through a firewall to prevent malicious access. This hardening method will close the open gateway caused by the lack of traffic rules and minimize exposure to external threats.

### 3. **Multifactor Authentication (MFA)**
Implement MFA organization-wide, particularly for sensitive systems such as databases and admin panels. MFA can stop unauthorized access even if login credentials are compromised. This directly mitigates the risk of brute-force or password-based attacks.

## Part 2: Explain Your Recommendations

### 🔹 Why These Tools and Methods Are Effective

- **Password Policies**: Strong password protocols protect against brute force attacks and credential stuffing. Preventing shared or reused passwords reduces the risk of one compromised credential affecting multiple systems. This practice should be enforced through technical controls (e.g., password complexity and history settings) and employee training.

- **Firewall Maintenance and Port Filtering**: Regular firewall rule audits and port filtering ensure that only necessary network traffic is permitted. This significantly reduces the attack surface and blocks unwanted traffic. Firewall configurations should be reviewed monthly, or after any major system update or incident.

- **Multifactor Authentication (MFA)**: MFA introduces an additional layer of protection that stops attackers who may have stolen or guessed a password. It’s especially effective against phishing, brute-force, and credential reuse attacks. MFA can be set up once and maintained with periodic reviews for effectiveness.

### 🔹 Implementation Frequency

- **Password policies**: Enforce organization-wide immediately and review quarterly.
- **Firewall rules and port filtering**: Review every month and after major changes or threats.
- **MFA**: Set up once and audit user compliance and authentication logs monthly.
